# LEGO Method of bagging tasks

## Definition / Formula

The LEGO Method for overcoming procrastination is a productivity strategy based on how modern LEGO sets are packaged: breaking a massive, overwhelming project into small, numbered bags that can be completed one at a time.

## Explanation

When you look at a major project as one giant task (like "Write Master's Thesis" or "Clean the Entire House"), your brain experiences cognitive overload. This triggers anxiety, which leads directly to procrastination. Accept that you cannot build the final structure in a single sitting.

Divide your massive project into highly specific, sequential phases—just like LEGO bags. Each "bag" should represent a micro-milestone that feels completely manageable.

Instead of: "Prepare quarterly tax report."
- Bag 1: Gather all receipts from Q3.
- Bag 2: Log receipts into the spreadsheet.
- Bag 3: Calculate total expenses.

### Open Only One Bag at a Time
When you sit down to work, your only objective is to finish the current numbered bag. Clear your workspace of everything else. Do not worry about Bag 4 while you are working on Bag 2. This hyper-focus lowers the barrier to entry, making it much easier to simply start.

### Celebrate the Micro-Builds
Modern LEGO sets give you a sense of accomplishment quickly; finishing Bag 1 usually rewards you with a completed minifig or the base of a vehicle. In your work, acknowledge the completion of each small bag. This releases dopamine, building the positive momentum you need to open the next bag.
