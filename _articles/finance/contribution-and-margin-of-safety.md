# Contribution and margin of safety

## Definition / Formula

An overview of unit economics of a product going from loss-making to profit-making and how managers can observe and react

## Explanation

### Formula cheatsheet
- $\text{contribution} = \text{Selling Price - Variable cost}$

- $bep_{\text{number of items}} = \frac{\text{Fixed cost}}{\text{Contribution per item}}$

- c/s ratio = $\frac{\text{Contribution per item}}{\text{Selling price of item}}$

- Breakeven revenue = $\frac{\text{Fixed cost}}{\text{c/s ratio}}$

- Margin of safety = $MoS =$ Total sales - Breakeven sales 

- Margin of safety ratio = $\frac{\text{Margin of safety}}{\text{Total sales}}$

- Net Profit = Total contribution - Fixed costs

- Net Profit margin = c/s raito * margin of safety

### Contribution

Business has 2 types of costs
- Fixed cost : Does not scale with a product upto a threshold
- Variable cost : Scales with a product linearly (mostly)

If we sell one product, we definitely price it higher than what it took to make it. The price that it took to make the product is called the variable cost. The difference between at the price on which item is sold and the price at which it was made is called `Contribution`

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{contribution} &= \text{Selling Price - Variable cost} \\
c &= s - v_c
\end{aligned}
$$

This "contribution" serves a purpose
- Till a certain point, it justifies the cost (fixed) spent to make and sell the product
- After a certain point, it directly leads to cash / profit
- This certain point is called `Breakeven point` (BEP)

### Breakeven point

At breakeven point, `Total fixed cost = Contribution per item * n` , `n = `number of items sold

$$

\begin{aligned}
bep_{\text{number of items}} &= \frac{\text{Fixed cost}}{\text{Contribution per item}}
\end{aligned}
$$

We can define a relationship that specifies that for each item sold, what portion of revenue is used to pay company's investment bills. This relationship or this ratio is called `c/s` ratio

$$

\begin{aligned}
c_s &= \frac{\text{Contribution per item}}{\text{Selling price of item}}
\end{aligned}
$$

In terms of money, that is, the amount of sales that justify the fixed costs can be calculated as well. This is done through $c_s$ ratio

$$

\begin{aligned}
bep_{\text{money}} &= \frac{\text{Fixed cost}}{\text{c/s ratio}}
\end{aligned}
$$
### Margin of safety

>[!note]
>Whenever we say `margin` in accounting, it means that we are comparing something with reference to its selling price $\implies \frac{\text{something}}{\text{selling price}}$

After our sales reach the breakeven sales, we can say that the contribution earnt is now acting as the profit of the company that is, the amount of money that can be reinvested into business or withdrawn. Mathematically

> Margin of safety = $MoS =$ Total sakes - Breakeven sales 

The ratio is calculated as 

$$

\begin{aligned}
mos_{\text{ratio}} &= \frac{\text{Margin of safety}}{\text{Total sales}}
\end{aligned}
$$

>[!tip]
>Essentially, it is just "% of sales that are pure profit"

### Net Profit Margin
Net profit is defined as `Total contribution - Fixed costs`. We can twist the formula to express the profit margin in terms of c/s ratio and margin of safety. The result is as follows

```
Net Profit margin = c/s raito * margin of safety
```


>[!note]
>Profit margin = $\frac{\text{Net profit}}{\text{selling pice}}$


### Why even care about this?
- If the Break-Even Point is the line between life and death for a business, the Margin of Safety tells you how many steps you can safely take away from that cliff.
- A high Margin of Safety (e.g., 50%) means you can survive market shocks—like a recession, a new competitor, or a supply chain failure. You have room to breathe and pivot.
- Low MoS results in every single lost customer being a danger. This triggers immediate cost-cutting (lowering Fixed Costs) or desperate marketing (trying to boost Sales).
