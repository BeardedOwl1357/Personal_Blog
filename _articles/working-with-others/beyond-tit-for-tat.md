# Beyond tit-for-tat

## Definition / Formula

You don't have to receive care in the exact form in which you give it. But you are allowed to want care, mutual effort and relationships that fulfil your needs.

You don't owe everyone unlimited investment, and nobody owes you identical behaviour.

Your responsibility is to recognise what people actually bring to your life, understand what they are willing to invest, and decide whether that relationship works for you.

## Explanation

I value friendships and groups where people look out for one another, anticipate small needs, and show consideration without always having to be asked. I enjoy being that person for others, and I appreciate receiving the same in return. But this is a value I bring to relationships, not an obligation I can impose on others or something I am automatically owed. People have different ways of expressing friendship, and they are entitled to their own expectations and boundaries.

I don't need to abandon my inclination to be considerate just because others may not share it to the same degree. Equally, I don't need to keep extending myself in the hope that others will eventually reciprocate. I can appreciate the kind of relationships I value, communicate what matters to me, and invest more in people with whom that consideration is mutual. 

I have a tendency to equate what I am getting in the relationships (romantic, friendships etc) and how I do this is by seeing whether what I am doing for others is being reciprocated to me or not. However, it assumes that people value the same things I do. If I really want to evaluate a relationship / friendship, I should evaluate them based on whether the relationship contributes positively to my life and whether there is mutual investment. There are 3 parameters

- **Contribution:** What positive things does this person bring into my life? **This does not mean that they offer the same thing that I do**. For example, if I help someone stay on track with things by reminding them and they help me get out of my shell by inviting me to plans, that is a positive contribution. 
    
- **Reciprocity:** Am I the only one making an effort to sustain this relationship? If I help and I get help, that is reciprocity. Do I feel like I am always doing things and they are not doing anything for me? If yes, this is a reciprocity issue. 
    
- **Compatibility:** Even if this person is a good person and cares about me, do we have compatible expectations? Do we value the same things? If I have to adjust with their quirks and they had to adjust with mine, is it doable? Can we be ourselves in this relationship without either person repeatedly feeling that they have to suppress important needs?

This is the healthier basis for evaluating friendships and romantic relationships than keeping a mental tally of who did what for whom.
