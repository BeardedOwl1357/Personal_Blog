# Leaning and outsourcing

## Definition / Formula

Leaning means that you keep the ownership of what needs to be done. Outsourcing is being entirely dependent on them and having no ownership. In the leaning case, you will somehow find a way of getting things done. In outsourcing, you don't even think of doing it on your own and just panic or find someone else who will take the ownership

## Explanation

### I want a relationship where she compensates the part that is missing in me" be careful with the frame : Difference between leaning and outsourcing

In relationships, reciprocal value is necessary. If partner is not available for a while (or for a long while), would things still get done albeit inefficiently? If yes, then it is leaning else outsourcing

Leaning tends to make you slightly less dependent over time ; not because you're trying to replace her, but because proximity transfers competence. You pick things up. You get better at the edges. After five years of leaning on her for X, you can do 30% of X yourself and you understand the other 70% well enough to know when it's going wrong. Outsourcing makes you more dependent over time ; or at best flat. After five years, you can do 0% of X and you don't even know when it's going wrong. You've not just delegated the task; you've delegated the ability to evaluate the task. That's the real cost.

Ultimately, leaning should result in
- Being able to handle the situation when the support is removed. If she were unavailable for 6 months, would the work still get done?
- Am I able to determine what or when something needs to be done? Have I learnt anything in the process? 

### Failure mode 2: The quiet resentment of the "competent" partner

The other version: you find someone genuinely strong : high-C, high-E, capable. She compensates beautifully. And then, over years, you notice something: **you resent her for being the one who does the things you can't.** Not consciously, not fairly : but the resentment is structural. Every time she organizes the trip, handles the social logistics, manages the emotional weather, you're reminded of the deficit. The relationship becomes a standing indictment of the part of you you've outsourced. And avoidant people don't process resentment by talking about it; they process it by **withdrawing**. So the relationship slowly hollows out from the inside while looking functional from the outside.

This is why "she compensates what's missing in me" is a _stable-looking but corrosive_ frame. It produces a relationship that works : until the ledger of unspoken deficits gets too heavy.

### Result
The centipede model (building a strong spine and small extensions on it) is your answer to the deadline version. **The reciprocal relationship frame is your answer to the partner version.** Both say the same thing: _I will build the spine myself, cheaply and incrementally, and then I will choose people to lean on and cover : not people to be the spine._

**For each one, run the six-month test.** Sort into "leanable" (fine to want help) vs. "collapse" (goes on the centipede list as a spine task).
